Tempeh math

Tempeh recipes are written for one batch size and your beans never weigh that much. Soak water, starter and the incubation clock all scale from one number - the dry weight of the beans.

Weigh the beans dry
Read this first This page does ratio arithmetic on labeled craft norms. It cannot tell you whether a ferment is safe to eat. Time, temperature and cleanliness decide that - follow a tested recipe and its safety guidance, and when a batch looks, smells or feels wrong, throw it out.

The problem it solves

A tempeh recipe that starts "500 grams of soybeans" breaks the moment your bag holds 375. The soak comes up short, the starter gets eyeballed, and the incubator runs at whatever temperature the spare room happens to be. Tempeh math scales the whole batch from the dry weight: soak water, cooked yield and vinegar, starter by the gram, and an incubation window that follows your actual temperature instead of the recipe author's.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the norms are not. The soak, yield, vinegar, starter and timing figures are flagged craft conventions, and the timing card is a labeled estimate, not a schedule for your kitchen. Bean variety, starter vitality and the real temperature inside your incubator decide the batch. This measures nothing about your ingredients or your ferment.
